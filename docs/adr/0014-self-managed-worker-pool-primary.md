# Self-managed worker pool is the primary orchestration path

Supersedes the primary/fallback ordering of ADR 0003: jobs are dispatched to workers that our own provisioner rents directly through Vast.ai's instance API, and the serverless endpoint becomes the designed-but-unbuilt alternative behind the same dispatcher seam. Everything else in ADR 0003 stands unchanged — warm floor of two Ready workers, ~30–40 ceiling as an accident guard, downscale to the floor after ~10 idle minutes, On-Demand only, one job per worker, auto-top-up with alerting.

The provisioner is a background service beside the dispatcher and recovery checks. Its scaling logic is three rules on a timer: below the warm floor → rent; a job waiting with no free worker carrying its model → rent one with that model's image variant (up to the ceiling); idle above the floor for ~10 minutes → destroy. Every rental passes an admission test before it may take jobs — the POC's live-proven readiness gate ported on-box, plus a timed mini-render that screens out too-slow machines — and a machine that fails is destroyed and remembered as bad. Because we choose each rental's image, warm-floor workers carry the full image while urgent burst rentals carry the single-model image the waiting job needs; because we choose placement, broken-machine avoidance is deterministic rather than dependent on router re-route behavior.

Why the reversal (2026-09-30, worker-image ticket #17): designing the image against serverless exposed structural rigidity — the router is model-blind (a mixed fleet needs one endpoint per image variant), workers cannot be admission-tested before taking traffic (stock PyWorker has no free-VRAM gate, so a poisoned machine takes the next job), and the autoscaler's knobs (`min_load`, `target_util`, `cold_mult`) only approximate the warm-floor policy by trial and error. Counting the serverless workarounds that would be built anyway — a route-protocol client in C#, a maintained PyWorker fork with an authored SeedVR2 benchmark, the endpoint/template IaC layer and its tuning — the build cost of the two paths is near-equal, while the pool path owns its riskiest code instead of depending on a platform launched late 2025. The POC's instance-selection and readiness-classification code is proven seed for the pool; nothing equivalent exists for the serverless protocol.

## Considered Options

- Stay serverless-primary and launch with a single full-image endpoint. Rejected — near-equal total work once the route client, PyWorker fork + benchmark and endpoint tuning are counted, and provisioning flexibility stays permanently capped.
- Serverless mixed fleet (full endpoint holding the warm floor + per-model burst endpoints). Rejected — one endpoint per image variant (seven with both precisions shipped), each separately created, tuned and monitored, plus real chooser logic in the dispatcher anyway.
- Self-managed pool primary (chosen) — full control of machine selection, image-per-rental and admission. The scaling logic stays small because prior decisions (one job per worker, warm floor of two, Postgres queue) already removed everything hard.
- Hybrid with both paths built. Rejected again per ADR 0003 — doubles the build for no launch benefit.

_Amended 2026-10-01 by ADR 0016 (provisioner ticket #23): the admission test's timed mini-render is dropped — admission is the readiness gate alone, and machine speed is predicted from hardware fingerprints plus accumulated job history instead of measured. The provisioner's full design (worker lifecycle, machine ledger, scouting, two-tier updates) lives in ADR 0016._
