# Naming/GTM economics: keyword-domain volume play vs. brand umbrella play

Date: 2026-10-02. Scope: profitability-versus-effort evidence for the two go-to-market directions under discussion — (A) descriptive/keyword-domain free-tier volume play ("imgupscaler"-style), (B) invented-brand premium play ("Magnific"/"Topaz"-style), (C) hybrid. Reliability of every figure is tagged **[self-reported]**, **[estimated]** (analytics services, third-party review/pricing aggregators, or our own arithmetic) or **[verified]** (SEC filing, acquirer announcement, vendor's own page).

**TL;DR.** The disclosed money in this niche is overwhelmingly on the brand side: Magnific (two people, zero ads, paid-only, invented name) self-reported $10.2M revenue in year one and $34.4M in year two at 90% gross margin after GPU costs, and was acquired by Freepik — which later renamed *itself* to Magnific; Topaz Labs (brand, 2005, bootstrapped) sold to Adobe in 2026 for ~$340M **[verified, SEC 10-Q]** on ~$48–50M revenue. The keyword-domain side shows huge traffic (imgupscaler.ai ~9M visits/mo, cutout.pro ~13M) but zero disclosed revenue anywhere — the operators are low-profile Hong Kong/anonymous entities, the family pattern (Vertexshare's imglarger+imgupscaler+BGEraser+…) monetizes at $9.99–$19.99/mo, and one family sold lifetime unlimited for $69 on AppSumo, which only works when marginal cost per job is near zero. That is the catch for us: those economics are **image** economics; a free tier of GPU **video** upscaling costs real money per use, and the closest video comp (TensorPix) throttles its free tier hard (720p cap, drip credits, one video at a time) and charges ~$0.40–$1.50 per output minute. SEO-wise, a new EMD in 2025-26 needs 6–12+ months to meaningful traffic in a SERP already occupied by Topaz, Canva, Adobe and incumbent EMDs, while Magnific's launch-to-revenue was days — but required a pre-built audience and a visibly superior product.

## 1. Revenue evidence per model

**Brand side.**

- **Magnific AI** (invented name, premium, paid-only at launch; founders Javi López + Emilio Nicolás, launched Nov 2023): revenue **$10.2M in 2024** and **$34.42M in 2025** (the 2025 figure includes post-acquisition sales from both websites), **90% gross margin after GPU costs**, 2 people, zero investment, zero ad spend — all from López's own X thread (https://x.com/javilopen/status/2049128517686919661) **[self-reported]**. Acquired by Freepik in May 2024, terms undisclosed (https://www.maginative.com/article/freepik-scoops-up-ai-image-upscaler-magnific/, https://x.com/javilopen/status/1787852344681787754) **[verified: acquisition; undisclosed: price]**. In April 2026 Freepik rebranded the whole company *to* Magnific, citing a combined **$230M ARR and ~1M paying subscribers** (https://thenextweb.com/news/freepik-rebrands-as-magnific, https://www.the-ai-corner.com/p/the-story-of-magnific) **[self-reported, company figures]**. Pricing: paid-only tokens/subscriptions, no free tier at launch; current aggregators list plans ~$20–$280/mo with ~$39/mo as the common entry point (https://costbench.com/software/ai-design-tools/magnific-ai/, https://shipsquad.ai/pricing/magnific-ai) **[estimated, third-party pricing pages]**.
- **Topaz Labs** (brand, founded 2005, bootstrapped): reportedly **~$48–50M annual revenue and 1M+ customers** at acquisition time (https://weandthecolor.com/adobe-topaz-labs-acquisition-what-actually-changes-for-creators-in-2026/210694) **[estimated, press reporting]**; Adobe agreed on 2026-06-25 to acquire it for **approximately $340M, primarily cash** — the price appears in Adobe's own Form 10-Q (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm, https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/adobe-acquires-image-and-video-enhancement-tool-maker-topaz-labs/) **[verified]**; deal closed 2026-09-23 (https://camerajabber.com/photography-news/adobe-completes-topaz-labs-acquisition-topaz-stays-a-standalone-brand/) **[verified]**. Pricing (2026, subscription-only since perpetual licenses ended Sept/Oct 2025): Gigapixel $149/yr, **Video AI $299/yr or $59/mo** (Pro $699/yr), all-apps Studio bundle $399/yr (https://www.myarchitectai.com/blog/topaz-ai-pricing, https://comparedge.com/tools/topaz-ai/pricing) **[estimated, third-party pricing roundups]**. Note for our niche: video upscaling is priced ~2× the image product.

**Keyword-domain side.**

- **Imglarger/ImgUpscaler family**: imglarger.com and imgupscaler.com both belong to **Vertexshare Limited**, a low-profile Hong Kong company whose stable also includes BGEraser, Object Remover, ImagetoCartoon, AnyWebp, FaceSwapper (https://vanceai.com/posts/imglarger-review, https://theresanaiforthat.com/ai/vertexshare/, https://vertexshare.com/) **[verified: common ownership; the family pattern is real]**. Pricing: Imglarger free 100 credits/mo, Premium $9.99/mo, Business $19.99/mo; ImgUpscaler Premium $9.90/mo (500 credits), Business $19.90/mo "no limit" (https://www.softwaresuggest.com/imglarger, https://www.capterra.com/p/264909/ImgUpscaler/) **[estimated, software directories mirroring vendor pages]**. **No revenue figure has ever been published anywhere we could find** — no Indie Hackers entry, no founder posts, nothing **[absence of data, searched 2026-10]**. Img.Upscaler also ran an AppSumo **$69 lifetime-unlimited deal** (34 reviews, 4.15 stars; sales volume undisclosed) (https://appsumo.com/products/imgupscaler/) **[verified: deal exists]** — selling unlimited forever at $69 is only rational when per-image GPU cost is negligible.
- **imgupscaler.ai** (the *other* imgupscaler — a different operator, "LinoCut AI", WHOIS behind Domains-By-Proxy, ScamAdviser trust 15/100): **8.94M visits/mo (Mar 2026)**, up from 6.42M (Nov 2025); 47% direct / 40% Google; #2667 rank in India (https://www.semrush.com/website/imgupscaler.ai/overview/, https://www.scamadviser.com/check-website/imgupscaler.ai, https://gridinsoft.com/online-virus-scanner/url/imgupscaler-ai) **[estimated, Semrush panel data]**. Meanwhile the original imgupscaler.com sits at ~3.4M visits/mo (https://www.similarweb.com/website/imgupscaler.com/) **[estimated]** — i.e. an anonymous clone on a sister TLD out-trafficked the incumbent EMD. No revenue data for either.
- **upscale.media** (PixelBin's keyword satellite): free 3 credits on signup + 1 image/day anonymous; paid from $9.99/mo for 100 credits, yearly 1,500 credits ≈ $25.49/yr (https://www.upscale.media/pricing, https://www.tooljunction.io/ai-tools/upscale-media) **[verified: vendor pricing page; estimated: review-site details]**. Traffic majority-organic (54% of desktop visits) (https://www.similarweb.com/website/upscale.media/) **[estimated]**. - **Bigjpg** (early EMD-ish upscaler, ~2017): free with limits, Pro ~$14.99/mo or $79/yr, $99 lifetime; older tiering $6/2mo–$22/yr (https://reviewramble.com/bigjpg-ai-upscaler-review/, https://aiparabellum.com/bigjpg-ai/) **[estimated, review sites]**. No revenue data ever published **[absence of data]**.
- Searches of Indie Hackers, TrustMRR-style trackers and Acquire.com surfaced **no public MRR reports and no disclosed exits for any keyword-domain upscaler** — the only upscaler exits on record are the two brand ones above (https://www.indiehackers.com/post/imgupscale-your-new-ai-image-upscaler-efb1935c6b is a launch post with no numbers) **[absence of data]**.

## 2. Traffic→money conversion for the volume play

- **Freemium benchmarks (2025-26):** visitors→free-signup ~9%; free→paid median ~5.5%, "good" 3–5%, top decile 8–12%; ~1 in 4 freemium products converts under 2.5%; AI-native products convert slightly better (good 6–8%, great 15–20%) (https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/, https://userpilot.com/blog/saas-average-conversion-rate/, https://www.productgrowth.blog/calculators/freemium) **[estimated, industry benchmark reports]**.
- **What ~9M visits/mo plausibly earns:** our arithmetic, not a source — visits ≠ users, and tool-site visitors skew anonymous one-shot (71% bounce on imgupscaler.ai **[estimated, Semrush]**). At $9.90/mo ARPU: if 0.1% of visits become a paying month that's ~$88K/mo (~$1M/yr); at 0.5% it's ~$440K/mo. The Vertexshare/LinoCut operators' behavior (family of ~8 sites, $69 lifetime deals, anonymous ownership, zero revenue bragging) is consistent with the low end per site, made worthwhile by running many sites off one model stack **[estimated — our inference]**.
- **The niche-specific cost catch:** every free use burns GPU money, and the observed mitigations are all throttles: Magnific's founders launched **paid-only explicitly because the workflow is GPU-heavy and they "could not afford to give it away"** (https://www.the-ai-corner.com/p/the-story-of-magnific) **[self-reported]**; upscale.media gives 3 credits + 1 anonymous image/day; Bigjpg free tier gets slow queues, size caps and watermark-free only on paid **[estimated, review sites above]**. For **video** — our product — the closest comp TensorPix caps free at 720p input, drip "weekly login credits" (~5 min of video), 1 GB storage, one video at a time, and prices paid usage at ~1 credit ≈ 2 min of output with extra credits at $0.80–$3.00/credit, i.e. **~$0.40–$1.50 charged per output minute** (https://tensorpix.ai/usecase/free-video-upscaler, https://kjetilfuras.com/tensorpix-review, https://www.aiarty.com/ai-video-enhancer/tensorpix.htm) **[verified: vendor free-tier page; estimated: credit math from reviews]**. The image-volume play's "give away millions of jobs, convert 1%" math does not transplant to video: per-job cost is minutes of GPU, not a fraction of a second.
- **AI-directory channel:** There's An AI For That ~785K–2M visits/mo, Toolify ~44–300K; directory visitors reportedly convert 3–5× better than social traffic; founders typically submit to 10–20 directories at launch, ~15 min each (https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-directories/, https://launchboosts.com/blog/best-ai-tool-directories-2026, https://thesaasdir.com/blog/best-directories-ai-tools/) **[estimated, directory-marketing posts — treat the conversion multiple as promotional]**. Cheap, worth doing under either direction; not a business by itself.

## 3. Effort/time profile per model

**A — ranking an EMD in 2025-26.**

- EMDs still get picked up fast for their one keyword — they need ~60% lower domain rating to crack the top 10 and hold ~25% of top-3 spots in one study — but 70% of EMDs rank for *only* their primary keyword, so each additional keyword means another domain or real content work (https://www.digitalleverage.ch/en/blog/study-exact-match-domains, https://www.seoexamples.com/p/exact-match-domains-how-emds-still) **[estimated, SEO-vendor studies]**.
- Time-to-traffic on a brand-new domain: 3–6 months before stable movement, **6–12 months to meaningful organic traffic**, 12–18 in competitive niches; only **1.74% of newly published pages reach Google's top 10 within a year** (down from 5.7% in 2017) and the average top-10 page is 2+ years old (https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/, https://factoryjet.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-2026-month-by-month-timeline) **[estimated, Ahrefs data study + agency timelines]**.
- The 2024-25 helpful-content/core updates hit thin tool-adjacent sites hard: publishers reported 30–90% traffic losses; thin content and unreviewed AI-generated pages are the stated target (https://crowdo.net/blog/helpful-content-update-traffic-loss-2025, https://www.plerdy.com/blog/google-helpful-content/) **[estimated, SEO-industry reporting]**. A one-page GPU tool with no content depth is exactly the profile at risk.
- And the "video upscaler" SERP is no greenfield: the current first page is Topaz, Canva, ElevenLabs, upscale.media, TensorPix, Morph Studio (observed in search results, 2026-10) **[verified by inspection]**. Solo-dev feasibility: the mechanics (EMD + landing pages + directories + link building) are one-person work, but it is months of content/SEO grind before signal, with GPU cost bleeding on every free use in the meantime.

**B — a Magnific-style launch.**

- What it actually took: a visibly-better product (latent-diffusion "creative upscaling" that competitors didn't have), **18 months of prior audience-building on X** by López, a 2-week throttled waitlist where hand-picked creators posted before/afters, 30K+ signups in 24h, 725K registered users in 5 months — and paid-only from day one, so time-to-first-revenue was effectively **days** (https://www.the-ai-corner.com/p/the-story-of-magnific) **[self-reported via press retelling]**. Two people total; ~50% of the launch codebase written with Cursor/Claude **[self-reported]**.
- The honest read: the launch mechanics (waitlist, creator seeding, X/Product-Hunt posts) are cheap and solo-feasible; the two hard prerequisites — a differentiated result people screenshot, and an audience or seeding channel — are not free. Without an existing audience, substitute: seeding free access to niche creators (retro-footage restorers, AI-video communities) and launch posts; time-to-revenue then depends on whether the output is visibly better than Topaz/TensorPix, not on SEO timelines.

**C — hybrid.** PixelBin (brand) runs upscale.media/erase.bg etc. as keyword satellites feeding the brand **[verified: same owner]**; Freepik(→Magnific) stacks many tools under one brand. Hybrid costs are additive but staged: brand home first, satellites later as marketing pages rather than separate products — satellites inherit the brand's model backend, so the extra cost per satellite is a landing page + domain, matching the Vertexshare pattern but with a brand to retreat to.

## 4. Durability

- **Volume/EMD side is structurally exposed:** (a) algorithm risk — the 30–90% HCU losses above hit exactly this site profile; (b) clone risk — demonstrated in-niche by imgupscaler.**ai** (anonymous, trust-score 15/100) out-trafficking imgupscaler.**com** 8.9M to 3.4M visits/mo on the same name **[estimated, Semrush/Similarweb]**; a keyword name is copyable by definition, and defending it means outranking yourself-lookalikes forever; (c) soft decline visible in the cohort — cutout.pro slid from ~15.1M visits (Aug 2025) to ~12.9M (Jan 2026) (https://cw.semrush.com/website/cutout.pro/overview, https://zh.semrush.com/website/cutout.pro/overview) **[estimated]**. No keyword-domain upscaler has a recorded exit **[absence of data]**.
- **Brand side has the recorded moats and exits:** Topaz, founded 2005, survived 20 years of model turnover and exited to Adobe at ~$340M **[verified, SEC]**, with Adobe keeping the *brand* alive post-close **[verified]**; Magnific's brand was strong enough that the acquirer renamed itself after it two years later (https://thenextweb.com/news/freepik-rebrands-as-magnific) **[verified: the rebrand happened]**. Brand-side risk is different in kind: product-quality churn (Topaz's 2025 move to subscription-only angered its base **[estimated, review-site commentary]**) and model-level competition, not SERP volatility.
- Both disclosed exits and all disclosed revenue in this niche sit on the brand side; the volume side's returns are opaque, which cuts both ways — it may hide decent cash flow, but nobody on that side has ever shown numbers worth bragging about **[inference from sections 1–2]**.

## What this means for us

| | A — keyword-domain volume | B — brand umbrella | C — hybrid (brand home + keyword satellites) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue potential (evidence)** | No disclosed revenue anywhere; our arithmetic on 9M visits/mo at benchmark conversions spans ~$1M–$5M/yr per site, and reaching that traffic is itself the unproven step **[estimated]** | The only disclosed numbers: Magnific $10.2M yr-1 / $34.4M yr-2, 90% GM **[self-reported]**; Topaz ~$50M/yr, $340M exit **[verified]** | Undisclosed, but both live specimens (PixelBin, Freepik/Magnific) chose it after establishing a brand |
| **Upfront effort** | Low build (one tool page), but content/SEO corpus + directory/link work from day one; free tier needs cost throttles engineered in | Higher: output must be visibly better than Topaz/TensorPix, plus launch seeding (waitlist, creators, X/PH) | B's upfront cost first; satellites are cheap add-ons later (domain + landing page on the same backend) |
| **Ongoing effort** | Perpetual SEO maintenance + free-tier GPU bill + clone defense | Product quality + community presence; no SEO treadmill | Both, but satellites are marketing pages, not products |
| **Time-to-first-revenue** | 6–12+ months to meaningful organic traffic on a new domain **[estimated, Ahrefs]**; paying GPU costs for free users throughout | Days-to-weeks if the demo lands (Magnific: paid-only from day 1) **[self-reported]**; indefinite if it doesn't | Same as B for first revenue; satellites add later |
| **Durability / moat** | Weak: 30–90% algorithm-update losses in this site profile; same-name TLD clones demonstrated in-niche; no recorded exits | Strong: 20-yr Topaz → Adobe; acquirer renamed itself to Magnific; both exits are here | Brand moat + diversified acquisition channels; satellite loss is survivable |
| **Fit with one-brand-many-offerings roadmap** | Poor: each offering needs its own domain and its own SEO climb; nothing accrues to a shared name | Natural: offerings stack under the brand (Topaz: 8 apps, one bundle; Magnific: one subscription, many models) | Natural: brand carries the roadmap, satellites harvest keyword demand per offering |

Two niche-specific facts should weigh on the choice more than the generic A-vs-B literature. First, the volume play's economics were proven on **images**, where a free job costs a fraction of a cent and $69-lifetime-unlimited deals are viable; **video upscaling's per-job GPU cost is the founders-of-Magnific reason to not give it away**, and the one video-native comp (TensorPix) runs a throttled free tier priced at ~$0.40–$1.50 per charged output minute — any free tier we offer needs those same throttles (res caps, drip credits, single concurrency) engineered from the start. Second, every disclosed dollar and both exits in this niche sit on the brand side, while the keyword side is anonymous operators who have never published a number — absence of evidence, but consistent. The evidence favors B-or-C over pure A for a video product with a one-brand-many-offerings ambition; the launch-mechanics prerequisite (a visibly superior demo plus a seeding channel) is the honest price of that path, and C defers the satellite question until there is a brand for satellites to feed. The choice is the human's.
